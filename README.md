# FS25 Sandbox Hammer

A hand tool that makes the world hold still where you tell it to, and a few objects you can throw heavy machinery at instead of opening the construction screen.

> basically just one by one replacing the construction screen with things you can actually throw heavy machinery at XD

The idea behind all of it: the game has to be exactly as annoying as the developer meant it to be. Some people enjoy tractoring pallets around every day. Others had fun with it "once", and then it became a chore, and real life has enough of those. So nothing here is forced on anyone: you buy it, you drag it, you fix it, or you do not.

## Fair warning: this whole mod is wrestling the game's physics engine

Everything in here looks simple ("it just stays put", "it just hangs on the bridge") and is a fight with an engine that was built for tractors on flat fields. The fight is worst on a reload, so here is what we know, in the order it hurt.

**Why a reload is the dangerous moment**
- The savegame keeps the poses things were in, including a chain sagging over a canyon. At a load the game makes its joints AT those poses, and the solver closes any joint error in ONE physics step: a gap of 4.7 m is closed at 280 m/s. That is the catapult. Pieces that "fly" at a load are almost always this.
- The game runs 4 s of physics before the first frame (`BaseMission.preSimulateTime`), and `CURRENT_MISSION_START` comes after it. Anything we set up there is 4 s too late: the chain has already sagged through whatever it was supposed to rest on. So the pins, the unbury, the level, the re-seat, the second joints and the steel profiles are all done on `SAVEGAME_LOADED`, which comes before it, in that order.
- Terrain the player raised under a piece puts it INSIDE the ground at the next load, and the physics throw it out (`unbury`). A piece that moved while pinned was saved off its pin (`level`, the keeper). A reload that "fixes" a stretched link by moving things must never move a pinned piece (it dragged the end piece closer at every load: the bridge was 0.27 m shorter after each restart).
- When something is thrown anyway, the speed guard slows it, the home memory (`bridgeHome.xml`) puts it back, and the log says which piece, how fast and where. The log is the evidence; read it before believing a theory.

**Why "frozen" is not the same as "pinned", and why neither is a wall**
- Freezing makes a body static: its collision is lost for compound vehicles and its joints die (a wagon comes apart, wheels vanish). So anything with joints (the bridge pieces, the poles) is only PINNED: an invisible joint to a kinematic helper body.
- A joint is a soft constraint. Enough force (a winch, a tractor, a chain pulling, something overlapping) stretches it. So the pieces are heavy while pinned (the game clamps a vehicle's mass to the `maxMass` in its XML: a 30 t pin mass needs a 40 t cap), and a keeper puts a pinned piece back on its helper when it is more than 3 cm off. If you see a pole jitter, the keeper and a force are arguing (`sbBridgeKeep off` shows who is winning).

**Engine facts we paid for**
- A compound body takes 40 children at most: long beams and cables are rows of 4 m units, 32 per body. A collision shape cannot be scaled, only the look can.
- A vehicle gets ONE attacher joint per pair: the second pair of a bridge link is a joint of our own, made after the attach, loose after a load.
- Collision groups and masks work in both directions: a mask with the wrong bit removed made poles passable for the player (0xfe2ffb83) instead of for vehicles (0xfe3efb83).
- The hands lift by `getTotalMass()`, which counts everything hitched: a free grate is 580 kg, the same grate in a chain is 6 t.
- A debug line lives one frame (draw from both update and draw, use pure colours), a physics query before the physics are ready misbehaves, the game's Lua has no `goto`, other mods' globals are invisible (publish through `g_currentMission`), and two hyphens in an XML comment break the file.

**What is still not solved**
- Anything that pulls hard on a pinned piece (a wire between two poles, a tractor on the chain) can still move it a few cm for a moment before the keeper puts it back.
- The steel bars are rigid. Bendy cables with real physics would be nicer, and would multiply every problem above.

## The Sandbox Hammer

Swing it at something (4 m range). What happens depends on what it hits:

| you hit | what happens |
|---|---|
| a log, a pallet, a ball, any single loose object | **frozen** in place (a static body). Swing again: thawed. |
| a vehicle with wheels, or one made of several bodies (a wagon) | **pinned**: an invisible locked joint to a kinematic helper body in the world. The vehicle stays dynamic and whole. Swing again: released. |
| a selling plate | **pinned** too, so it keeps its collision and its triggers (see below). |
| a hall or barn with a mouse sticker | the whole hall is frozen or thawed. A door gets a knock, it opens or closes. |

A vehicle the Hammer holds (pinned or frozen) is drawn **blue** on the map and the minimap, so you can see at a glance which of your machines are fixed.

The aiming cube tells you the state: green = free (a swing would freeze it), red = frozen or pinned, magenta = shift-frozen, grey = the hammer leaves it alone (the terrain and animals: a horse is a one-body vehicle, but freezing one is not a thing).

* **Left Shift + swing**: shift-freeze. The object stays frozen when a hall is thawed or `sbFreezeAll` thaws everything. Only another shift swing lets it go.
* **B**: bind. Pick one object, press B, pick another: the second is tied to the first, to move as one.
* **R**: rotate the bound pair 90 degrees around the target point.
* Everything is saved with the savegame (`sandboxtools.xml`) and found again after a load: a vehicle by its unique id, a log or tree by the engine's saveable split shape id (the same one the forestry missions use), and anything else by the nearest object to where it was.

### Why pinned and frozen are different things

Changing a body's type (dynamic to static and back) creates a **new physics body**. For a single, wheel-less object that is fine. For a wagon it is a disaster: the wheel shapes and the joints between its bodies die ("Wheel shape not found", it comes apart at the next pull). And a frozen (static) body loses its collision with wheels, and loses the trigger shapes that are part of it. That is why the Hammer pins anything with wheels, several bodies, or a job to do. A pin never changes the body type, so releasing it later cannot throw anything.

Frozen objects remember their mass and centre of mass (a thawed ball used to come back at 372 kg instead of 3 kg). Logs and trees are the exception: they get both from their wood.

### Console commands

* `sbFreezeAll [radius] [thaw]`: freeze (or thaw) everything loose around you, default 12 m. For building a hall.
* `sbMend [radius]`: a vehicle that came apart gets put back together (the game's own teleport pattern).
* `sbFreezeParts [on|off]`: freeze every body of a multi-body vehicle, not only its root (off by default, it breaks wagons).

## The hand tool picker (key 1)

Key 1 opens a picker instead of "yet another toggle" (`scripts/handTools/ToolPicker.lua`, action `SANDBOX_TOOL_PICKER`): tiles with the shop pictures of the tools you carry, tile 1 the empty hands, the one in your hand marked. Click a tile or press its number to take it; ESC, key 1 again, or a click beside the tiles closes it. While it is open the on-foot input is locked (the game's own `PlayerInputComponent:lock()`) and the mouse cursor is shown. The game's own key 1 ("switch hand tool", the cycle) collides with it: the picker switches that one binding off for the session with the engine's own `disableBinding` (the key 2 side of the cycle and the toggle on 3 stay; not saved; the log says `[ToolPicker] registered; N binding(s) of the game's hand tool cycle switched off`). The key is an ON-FOOT action: in a vehicle 1 and 2 stay the cruise control speed keys. Untested in the game.

## Number keys for the hand tools

Scrolling through hand tools is a pain, so slot keys (`scripts/handTools/ToolSlots.lua`, the actions `SANDBOX_TOOL_SLOT_1..10` in modDesc): **Alt+1 ... Alt+8 and Alt+0 (slot 10)**. Slot N is the Nth tool you carry, in the order the game's own cycle key walks them (slot 1 is the empty hands); the same key again puts the tool away (back to the hands); a message says which tool you took, or that the slot is empty. The plain digits are taken by the game (1 and 2 cycle, 3 toggles, 9 the map, 0 the horn), and Alt+9 by the minimap mod, so **slot 9 has no key until you set one** in the controls menu (every slot can be rebound there: "Hand tool slot N"). The events belong to the player's input component and are hidden from the key help. Known limit: the order is the game's carried order, so buying or selling a tool moves the slots after it.

## Lifting a bridge piece with a crane (pipe layer, telehandler)

Every grate, start piece and end piece has a red lifting lug lying flat at its middle and three **lifting joints** there (`lift1..3` in the generator, `LIFT_TYPES`): input attacher joints of the base game's types `wheelLoader`, `telehandler` (both `hardAttach`, as a pipe's top joint in FSM_Pipe_Layer_Pack) and `bigBag`. A pipe layer's spreader (`pipeTraverseBig`, `pipeTraverseSmall`) holds a pipe at ONE point, the middle, with a payload joint of type wheelLoader or telehandler, and a telehandler with a big bag lifter takes bigBag: so the game's own attach lifts our pieces too, with no implement of our own. **Two ways to hook one:** drive the machine so its hook or spreader hangs over the lug and press the game's attach key; or walk up with the leash (the winch of WinchTow takes the joint you look at). The bridge's own eyes stay joints 1 and 2 (BridgeLink counts input joints by index and only treats joints of the `trailer` type as eyes); a pinned or locked piece has to be unpinned first. Untested in the game.

## Baking a bridge piece into a placeable (prototype)

A pinned bridge piece is a vehicle held by joints; **baked** it is a static placeable twin (`scripts/vehicles/BridgeBake.lua`): no body, no joint, no keeper, no pin helper, saved by the game in `placeables.xml` and loaded with the savegame before the physics run. The twin is made by `tools/make_selling_plate.py` (`write_static_twin`) from the SAME shapes file as the vehicle: `vehicles/<name>/<name>_static.i3d` (the look, the hull as a static collision shape, the link rings and the clips as pure looks) and `<name>_placeable.xml` (a `simplePlaceable`, not for sale, listed in modDesc storeItems because the loading data looks the item up by file). **Console:** `sbBridgeBake [all]` bakes the bridge piece nearest to you (all: every pinned piece): the twin is loaded and FINALIZED (`Placeable:finalizePlacement`: physics, placeable system, unique id, save; without it the twin has no collision and cannot be found again) first, and the vehicle is deleted only after that; `sbBridgeUnbake [all]` spawns the vehicle again at the placeable's pose, lifted clear of the ground (the unbury's five-point terrain check) and deletes the placeable. **Not done yet:** nothing calls it by itself on a freeze; the hitches are not remembered (an un-baked piece is loose); baking a start or end piece takes the steel profiles and kerbs with it (they are built while those two are pinned vehicles), so bake GRATES first; poles and anchors have no twin. `sbBridgeRings ghost|solid` sets the link rings to collide with nothing (a test of the theory that overlapping rings make a chain fly). Nothing here touches the frozen objects and assemblies (the logs).

## The leash

A hemp rope in the hand (Sandbox Engineering, 25 EUR, `handTools/leash/`, `HandToolSandboxLeash.lua`): the winch of FS25_WinchTow, published on `g_currentMission.winchTow`, in a tool of its own. A click hooks the joint or the log you look at, the next click lets it go; with wires laid between bridge pieces and nothing hooked, the button HELD reels them in; Q unhitches everything. It needs FS25_WinchTow (the tool says so when it is missing) but NOT the horse mod: it is a clone of the horse mod's leash without the horses, kept apart on purpose (owned items are saved by their xml path, so moving the horse mod's leash would orphan the one you own). The shop picture is the rope of the mod icon (`make_leash_icon.py` (in ~/fs25_tools/icons, not in this repo)). Hand tools are a scrolling pain; the horse halter and the harness are becoming vehicles, which will free slots.

## The selling plate

A flat iron grid in an iron frame, 3 x 4 m, 800 kg, with three towing eyes on one short edge. It is a heavy object in the world: drag it with a tractor on its eyes, or with the hand winch (FS25_WinchTow), then **pin it with the Hammer where it lies**. A loose plate buys nothing. A pinned plate buys, at the base-game prices:

* crops tipped from a trailer onto the grid,
* pallets and bales on it,
* logs on it (stand next to it, the usual sell-wood key),
* pallets of potatoes, beetroot, carrots, parsnips and wool (the base game's market lists these next to its categories).

A pile up to 3 m high counts. The game's pallet trigger only decides about a pallet once, when it enters; the plate tells it again every second about the full pallets lying on it, so one that was there before the plate was pinned still sells. **Empty pallets** that stay on a pinned plate for 4 seconds are removed (the base game's trigger only takes pallets that hold something). Strapped pallets are left alone.

Put it on a factory's pallet spawn area and the factory's output lands on it and sells by itself: while it is pinned, the game's "is this spot free" test treats the plate as free ground. Yes, that is a little bit of a cheat. Mods that do the same with a placeable exist, and a greenhouse has a flag for it. If it feels too generous: the price is a number in `vehicles/sellingPlate/sellingPlate.xml` (`priceScale` on each trigger, `litersForFullPriceDrop` on the station).

Built for a world that is generated from scratch and shaped with other tools: opening the construction screen to place a selling point is the boring part. Also handy on a map that has no universal selling point.

A mouse with a funnel (it takes the crops) is engraved into the outer side of the plate's frame, on both sides, near the rear end (1.2 mm deep, 9.5 cm tall, real geometry: the grooves are cut into the face, no texture); the bridge pieces carry the mouse with the wrench. `assets/mouse_<name>_engraving.svg` is the line art (the wrench mouse is traced from the original picture instead: `assets/mouse_wrench_original.webp` through potrace into `assets/mouse_wrench_trace.geojson`, which wins over the hand-drawn svg); `tools/make_mouse_engraving.py <name>` (needs shapely and mapbox_earcut in a venv) turns the strokes into `tools/<name>_engraving.json`, `tools/make_selling_plate.py` cuts them into the frame bars. Mice crawling out of the grid might come one day.

## The bridge grate

The same grid and frame as the plate, no selling station, and a hitch at the other end: three towing eyes at the front, three hitches at the rear (the game's own attacher joints, type trailer, tight at 3 degrees). Two short end pieces (1.6 m) go on the banks: the **start piece** has three hitches (green) for the first grate and one iron ring at the other end, the **end piece** has three eyes (orange) for the last grate and one hitch at the other end. The ring is to drag the whole assembly from. Pin those two with the Hammer, then hitch the grates between them end to end with a tractor or the hand winch (hook the eye, look at the next grate's hitch, click): two or three grates over a gorge. The Hammer pins every bridge piece, and anything hitched to something, instead of freezing it: a frozen body would lose its attach joints. The game saves attachments, so the assembly should be there after a reload. A first test of the idea of a rope bridge: it is stiff for now, the rope comes later (a joint between two eyes).

The game joins two vehicles with one attacher joint at most, so a second physics joint of ours (`scripts/vehicles/BridgeLink.lua`) takes the other pair of eye and hitch as soon as the attach has pulled the pieces together, and is taken away again when they are unhitched. **Load order (why a bridge no longer sags on a reload):** the game runs 4 s of physics (`BaseMission.preSimulateTime`) after a savegame is loaded and before `CURRENT_MISSION_START`, where the pins used to be restored: the bridge hung on single joints for those 4 s. Now the pins (and BridgeLink's second joints of the saved attaches) are made on `MessageType.SAVEGAME_LOADED`, which comes BEFORE that pre-simulation; log lines `pinned N/N vehicle(s) BEFORE the game's pre-simulation` and `N second joint(s) rebuilt BEFORE ...`. Frozen objects, assemblies and stickers still restore at CURRENT_MISSION_START (they need raycasts). **Mass:** the start and end pieces weigh 600 kg like a grate while they are free (you can drag them, the hand can lift them), and become 3 t while the Hammer pins them (`HandToolHammer.PIN_MASS_T`): the bridge hanging off them cannot pull them off the bank; the pin is restored when a savegame loads and the weight with it. **Winch latches (yellow):** every piece carries yellow staples with a ring on its side bars (two per side on a grate at 0.9 m from the middle, one per side on the short end pieces): joint type `winchLatch` (registered in modDesc, joints 3 to 6 of a grate, after the two hitches), a type no eye accepts, so the game's attach and BridgeLink (`otherPair` skips them) never use them, while the tractor winch and the hand winch can hook them as a "winch latch" (a rope or pull from any machine, later the poles and the guide wires). Made by `tools/make_selling_plate.py` (`LATCH_Z`, `LATCH_R`). **Connection flags:** over both joints of every hitched pair within 30 m stands a coloured flag (a column of debug cubes, the top one bigger, as tall as the state is good: 5 joined, 3 closing, 2 pulled apart, 1 no joint, drawn with the same call as the Hammer's aim marker): green = joined and the points together, yellow = the second joint is being closed, orange = joined but pulled apart (more than 10 cm), red = no joint there (only one pair holds); the log says when a pair changes state (`[BridgeLink] A -> B: hitch ..., second joint ...`). Console `sbBridgeStatus [on|off]` switches the flags and lists every pair in the log. **Shop:** all of these are in their own Objects category "Sandbox Works" (modDesc `storeCategories`, made by `scripts/addStoreCategory.lua`), each with its own picture (`make_store_icons.py` (in ~/fs25_tools/icons, not in this repo): Blender renders the real i3d, flat things sit low in the tile); the winch pole and anchor are sold as a set (RandomFixes' "How many?" starts at 4). **Winch anchor:** a small plate (0.7 m, 60 kg free and carried in the hands, Pallets, 120) with one winch latch, an upright ring standing right on the plate (no pedestal: no lever arm), flat on the ground, the same eye, mouse and four bolts (shown once pinned), 3 t pinned: an anchor for a guy line or a winch pull where a pole is too much. Both parts come from `tools/make_winch_pole.py` (`PARTS`). **Placement guide (`sbBridgeGuide [on|off]`):** from a PINNED bridge start piece (the bridge goes on from its hitches; the poles and the end piece need not be pinned: they count wherever they stand, so the squares turn green as you drag them into place, and you pin them there) the debug lines show where things go, on the ground, following the terrain: the two poles beside the start piece (gold squares, 1.1 m, a cross where the post stands), a thin tick across the axis at the spans around the suggestion (centre to centre `1.6 + 4 n + 0.17 (n + 1)` m for n grates, up to 100 m), and for the n nearest to where you stand (or, standing at the start piece, the SUGGESTED one in orange: a horizontal terrain ray along the axis at the deck's height runs to the far hill, then steps up 0.5 m at a time until it passes over the top = the lip; a bank lower than the deck is found from the terrain heights: where the ground falls away and comes back up) the end piece's footprint (blue), the two poles beside it and a text with the grate count, the span and the ground height difference. With an end piece placed the whole layout AIMS at it (a start piece a few degrees off the end piece would put the squares metres beside the real poles; up to 10 degrees). The far side is THREE things, the end piece and two poles: a pinned end piece near one of the spots picks that span, its footprint turns green and its two poles go beside IT; a pinned pole there picks the span too and the square for its partner is drawn on the other side at the same distance; the text says "end piece placed / missing, N of 2 poles". The joint between a ring and its own grate has collision off, but a ring against the NEIGHBOUR grate collides (mask 0xfe3ffb83 includes the vehicle bit). A contact like that pushes a light ring against a 500 kg grate: the suspected reason chains go mad. `sbBridgeRings ghost` tests it. **What it costs (`sbBridgePerf [reset]`):** every per-frame step of BridgeLink (the flags, the home step, the profiles with lock and keeper, the guide wires, the snap, the speed guard, the draw phase) is timed with `getTimeSec`; the log line `[BridgeLink] cost: ...` every minute and the console command give ms per second, calls and the worst call per step, with the number of complete bridges, grates and hitched pairs next to it, so what grows with the number of bridges shows up in the log. **Jitter under a heavy vehicle (`sbBridgePinMass [grate t] [ring t]`):** a tractor of 10 t or more on a 580 kg grate joined to 40 kg link rings is a bad mass ratio for the solver, pinned or not (a pin is a soft joint). The console command sets the mass of every PINNED grate and of its rings live (`HandToolHammer.PIN_MASS_T.bridgeGrate`, `PIN_RING_T.bridgeGrate`; the game's `maxMass` clamp is lifted while pinned); not saved, so it is for finding the numbers (try `20 1`). **Reseat and pins:** the re-seat leaves a link alone when a PINNED piece lies behind it (its subtree is never moved: it dragged the pinned end piece and its pin towards the start piece at every load, the bridge 0.27 m shorter each time; log `reseat: N link(s) with a pinned piece behind them left as saved`). **No orange eye on the pole and the anchor any more:** they are carried by hand (120 and 60 kg, `supportsPickUp`) or lifted by their winch latches; the towing eye (a `trailer` input attacher joint) and the `attachable` specialization are gone from both vehicle types. The keeper also looks at TILT now (about 1 degree): a tall post under a wire's pull tips about its base without its origin moving. **Pin keeper:** (`sbBridgeKeep [on|off]`: off = it only looks and logs; the log line gives the drift vector and speed) a pin is a joint, soft under force; twice a second a pinned start piece, end piece or pole more than 3 cm from its pin's helper body is put back on it (pose, no velocity; `KEEP_M`), logged as `pinned X had drifted N m from its pin: put back`; start and end pieces weigh 30 t while pinned. **Hands:** the plate and free bridge pieces are lifted by the hands (`supportsPickUp`, the game's mass check lifted for them, pinned ones excluded); the log line `hands look at ...` says what the hands see. **Unbury (`sbBridgeUnbury`, and at every load):** the player can raise the terrain under a pinned piece; at the next load it then lies INSIDE the ground and the physics throw it out on the first step (a loose piece at 100 m/s, a pinned one shakes its whole chain). Before anything is pinned (`restorePinsEarly`, on SAVEGAME_LOADED) every bridge piece, pole and anchor is checked: five points of its footprint in its own orientation against the terrain height; a chain (what hangs on one piece) is lifted TOGETHER by its deepest burial plus 3 cm (capped at 3 m), every body by the same amount so no joint is stretched, and a pinned piece takes its pin helper along. A burial under 2 cm is left alone; a chain hitched to something that is no bridge piece is left alone (the log says so). The log line: `SandboxHammer: unbury: N piece(s) lay inside the ground ... lifted clear, by up to X m`. The console command does the same on demand (for terrain raised while the game runs). **The engine's compound limit (found 2026-11-17):** `Failed to create compound beamRoot with 69 children. Maximum is 40` in the log: a compound body takes 40 children at most, so a 68 m profile or cable got NO collision at all (the short ones worked). The engine cannot stretch a collision shape, so a long profile is a row of fixed units; they were 1 m (69 for 68 m), now 4 m (17 for 68 m: one body; the visual profile is ONE piece per body, stretched to its whole length (only the collision needs fixed units)), and a body is still capped at 32 units (`BEAM_CHUNK`, `WinchCable.CHUNK`). **Pole direction:** a pole snapped into its square is turned so its LOWER eye (and the top eye on the same side, local +x) points along the bridge towards the OTHER end (near poles along the line, far poles back along it, yaw `atan2(-dz, dx)`), so each guide wire runs between two eyes that face each other; the squares carry an arrow for it. A pole snaps from 0.5 m and from any angle (it is optics); a PINNED pole does not move: unpin it, let it snap, pin it again. **Home (`sbBridgeHome [on|off|now]`): a thrown piece is put back.** At a load, after the unbury and before the physics warm-up, every bridge piece, pole and anchor remembers its pose (all its bodies); the memory follows legitimate moves (a piece that rests 2 s somewhere else has a new home). Thrown means: faster than 40 m/s, a jump of more than 60 m between two looks (the game's own "reset to shop" teleport), or, in the first 20 s after a load, more than 5 m from home. It is put back with zero velocity (`[BridgeLink] home: <piece> was thrown (...): put back`), at most 3 times, then left. Whatever stood on a thrown piece stays where it fell: a tractor on a thrown bridge ends up in the ditch. **The homes are saved (`bridgeHome.xml`, in the savegame folder):** written whenever the game saves (`FSBaseMission.saveSavegame`, keyed by unique id: the pose of every body of every piece). At a load a piece that lies more than 20 m from its saved home was saved while thrown: it is put back at once, before the physics warm-up (`home: <piece> was saved N m from its last good place: put back`). The saved home is then its home. **Why a chain is thrown at a load, and the re-seat:** the solver closes a joint's error in ONE physics step, at about gap / step speed (a pair 4.68 m apart: 4.68 / 0.0167 = 280 m/s: the throws in the log). A chain over a canyon sags and stretches, the savegame keeps those poses, and at the next load the game makes its tight attach joints there. So before the first physics step (`SAVEGAME_LOADED`, after the unbury, before the second joints) every hitched pair is looked at from the roots of each chain outward: the child, with everything that hangs on it, is moved by the vector that brings its eye to a taut link's span (6 cm) from the ring's bar (`BridgeLink.reseatChains`; 2 cm to 25 m, translation only). Log: `[BridgeLink] reseat: N stretched link(s) pulled closed before the physics (up to X m)`. **The speed guard no longer clamps a fall** (gravity: a clamped fall looks like a feather): sideways and upward speed up to 12 m/s, a fall up to 60 m/s. **Profiles and vehicles (`sbBridgeBeams vehicles|novehicles`):** a pinned piece is held by a joint, not by a wall: a contact force shoves it (the end piece was pushed 40 cm over a minute by profiles that overlapped it and were rebuilt every 2 s). `novehicles` takes the VEHICLE bit out of every profile's collision mask at once (`setCollisionFilterMask`), so a bridge piece or a vehicle passes through them and only the player, the hands, a horse and the terrain touch them; `vehicles` (the default) puts it back. **Winch pole:** a shop vehicle (Pallets, 300) of a wide iron plate (the mouse with the anchor engraved on its side: `assets/mouse_anchor.png`, traced by potrace, `tools/make_mouse_engraving.py mouse_anchor`), an upright post and three yellow winch latches (two at the top on opposite sides, one low) and four anchor bolt heads (hex heads on round washers, dark steel) at the plate's corners (a node of their own: hidden until the Hammer pins the pole, `HandToolHammer.setPinVisuals`), made by `tools/make_winch_pole.py`. Free it weighs 120 kg (the hands tool lifts and carries up to 200 kg: `supportsPickUp`) and stands on its plate; or drag it by the orange eye on the plate (tractor or hand winch), put it where you want it, pin it with the Hammer: pinned it is 3 t (`PIN_MASS_T`) and held by the pin joint, so a winch can pull a bridge against it. Poles stand on both banks (up to three each) as anchors for the guide wires of a bridge too wide to cover otherwise. **Link rings:** every hitch is a blue ring (a body of its own, a second component of the piece) hooked on a U lug, and every eye is the same U lug: the component joint hangs the ring on its bar (it swings 80 degrees about the bar, tilts 25 and slides 2 cm), the game's attach (and our second joint) hooks the other piece's bar into the ring's far end with the same limits (`lowerRotLimit`/`lowerTransLimit` in the xml, `ROT_DEG`/`TRANS_M` in BridgeLink.lua). Two such links hold a grate against twisting and swinging but leave the leeway a chain has, so pieces need not lie flush on uneven ground, and a chain of grates can sag a little like a rope bridge. The link ring is a game component joint, so a savegame keeps it; after a load the game hitches the pieces again and BridgeLink rebuilds the second joint at once (log: `hitched again from the savegame`). Each end has two joints, the middle one is gone. **With wires (the way to do it):** hook a hitch of one piece with the hand winch and click the eye of the other: a steel wire is laid, nothing moves; do the same for the second pair; then reel both wires in with the winch keys (K) and the pieces are joined when both wires are short (see the WinchTow README). **Second pair by itself:** when two pieces are hitched with one pair and the other pair is within 2 m (`AUTO_PULL_M` in BridgeLink.lua), the loose piece swings the other pair closed on its own (the same swing as below), so one wire is enough for a bridge that is nearly in line; log line `the other pair is N m apart: the loose piece swings it closed`. **What the swing aims at:** a ring hangs loose from its lug bar, so the eye is brought to a taut link's span (6 cm) from the BAR, not to the hanging ring's own node, and the second joint is made when it is within 2 cm of that (the light ring follows the eye); the swing turns the whole side beyond the new joint (what hangs on the loose piece goes with it), and the other side when that one holds a pinned piece or a tractor. **A swing that stalls:** when the loose piece cannot move (both its ends are held: the last gap between two anchored halves of a bridge) the points stop getting closer; after 9 s, if they are within 35 cm, the second joint is made anyway, loose: its slack is the gap plus 3 cm, so it holds without pulling (log `the swing stalled at N m ... second joint made loose`). Farther apart it gives up after 25 s. **Watching the distance:** a hitched pair never stops looking for its other pair (every 0.2 s, `RESCAN_MS`): whenever the two points come within 2 m, whoever brought them there (you carrying the loose piece, the winch, a slide down the slope), the swing starts and the second joint is made when they meet; a swing that got nowhere in 25 s goes back to watching. **After a load the joints are loose first:** a second joint rebuilt from the savegame is made LOOSE at the offset it finds (`createJoint` with the real distance of the two nodes as its slack), because a tight one with the two nodes up to 14 cm apart threw a chain of eight grates off a slope in the pre-simulation (2026-11-15); the swing used to close them for real `LOAD_UPGRADE_MS` (6 s) after the world runs, but 15 swings at once flung a settled chain (2026-11-16), so `LOAD_UPGRADE` is now off: after a load the second joints stay loose at their offset (a few cm, the first joint of every link is tight) until you unhitch and hitch again. **A swing that keeps pushing:** when the gap has not shrunk by 5 mm the swing pushes harder, up to 12 times the asked turn over 4 s (`BOOST_MAX`, `BOOST_RAMP_MS`), and lifts the loose side a little (`BOOST_LIFT_MS`) so friction on the ground gives way; the log line shows `push xN`. The loose-joint fallback above now waits 9 s (`STALL_MS`). **Second pair by hand:** after the first eye and hitch are joined, hook the next hitch (or eye) with the winch and click: the rope takes the other free eye or hitch of the two pieces and the loose piece is swung about the first joint (at most 0.3 rad/s, 15 s) until the two points meet, then the second joint is made. If one piece is pinned, the other one swings; if both are pinned it says so. The first joint may tilt 25 degrees sideways for that (`lowerRotLimit="80 25 25"`); the second joint then holds it. Pinned pieces without wheels can be hooked by the winch (it refuses only pinned vehicles with wheels). **Reset:** the console command `sbBridgeReset [radius] [delete]` unpins and unhitches all bridge pieces around you (60 m by default), and with `delete` removes them. **Gather:** `sbBridgeGather` releases EVERY bridge piece on the map (flung ones included) and puts them down in a grid in front of you, 3 m apart.

## How the objects are made (for the next one)

* One **lean vehicle type** of its own (the base vehicle, `attachable` for the hitch eyes, and the job). Never `baseAttachable`: it brings a power take-off, folding, lights and a power consumer, and some of their keys answer "Please start the engine first!".
* **Eyes** are ordinary attacher joints, so a tractor hitch and the winch both take them.
* **Fixed** means pinned, not frozen, as above.
* **The job** (here: a base-game selling station) is built from the vehicle's own xml, like the game's selling-station placeable does it, and gated on "pinned or frozen".
* The model is written from scratch by `tools/make_selling_plate.py` (shapes file, collision hull, triggers), the shop picture by `make_plate_icon.py` (in ~/fs25_tools/icons, not in this repo).

Next on the list, if the mood strikes (none of it started): log building (logs standing upright on purpose, frozen), a wall and beam kit that stretches between two clicked surfaces without a gap, then a tack room, a stable, maybe a horse shower. Placeables are boring.

## Building the assets (for anyone else)

The i3d and shapes files in `vehicles/`, `models/` and the like are generated by the scripts in `tools/` (`make_selling_plate.py`, `make_winch_pole.py`, `make_beam_segment.py`, `make_mouse_engraving.py`); they write the GIANTS shapes format with the helpers of the sibling mod **FS25_HorseDynamics** (`tools/make_hitching_post.py`, `shapes_io.py`, `make_horse_jumps.py`), so check that mod out next to this one. The shop pictures are made by three small scripts that live outside this repo (`~/fs25_tools/icons`: Blender renders the i3d, ImageMagick crops it, the GIANTS Texture Tool makes the dds); they take the mod folder from `FS25_MOD` and the horse mod's tools from `FS25_HD_TOOLS`. The game does not need any of this: the generated files are committed.
